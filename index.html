    What your shop could sell for — and why it’s a range

    HVAC deals are priced on normalized earnings × a multiple. The multiple is not magic: it rises with recurring agreements, clean monthly books, a GM who runs the day, and growth — and falls with owner-dependence, install-only revenue, and customer concentration. This is an educational starting range, not a formal valuation or Quality of Earnings review.

    ↑ Moves the multiple up

    • Maintenance agreements with high renewal & documented visits
    • Service & replacement mix, not just new-construction install
    • GM + dispatch that work when you’re on vacation
    • Monthly financials that tie to tax returns, clean add-backs
    • Diversified customers, strong Google reputation, equipment-age data

    ↓ Moves it down (or adds an earn-out)

    • Owner is lead tech, lead salesperson & dispatcher in one
    • One builder/GC is 25%+ of revenue
    • Cash jobs, personal expenses through the business, tax-time-only books
    • License held only by the exiting owner
    • Aging fleet with big capex due in year one
    Before you quote a price to anyone: get a CPA/broker market valuation and, for larger deals, a Quality of Earnings review. A buyer will do one — you want to see your numbers through their eyes first. Also model after-tax proceeds: asset vs stock sale, seller-note risk, and working capital can swing what you keep by 15–30%.

    Use this as a starting conversation — then interview at least two advisors/buyers on that route before signing an exclusivity or LOI.

    Golden rules for any route: never sign a long exclusivity without a clear buyer list & timeline; never share full financials before an NDA; never accept a headline price without asking how much is cash at close vs earn-out / seller note / rollover; and keep running the business — a revenue dip during diligence is the #1 reason deals get re-priced.
